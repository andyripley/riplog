## Group Think

One could say that I am chronically online and that I spend too much time on Reddit. It has slowly replaced all of the BBS and Forums that used to exist in the world. While I try my best to get my news from multiple sources, that becomes harder when you want to discuss topics outside of politics. If you want to spend time discussing tech online, your options are essentially Reddit or a niche Discord server. This has several issues.

Reddit has had an issue with parroting the same jokes, phrases, thoughts, etc for years. This is nothing new in any online space but it's noticeably bad on Reddit where group think controls the algorithm. It encourages saying things that are popular, leaving out all forms of nuance along the way. This issue becomes interesting when you bring in the silos of sub-reddits.

Within the areas of tech that I care about (Linux, DevOps, WebDev, etc), there's a pervasive dismissiveness when it comes to projects that show any signs of AI usage. It's as if projects with an AGENTS.md have scarlet lettered themselves from being able to promote. I will concede that slop has increased (see the amount of portals on the AWS sub-reddit).

On the flip side, the vibe-coding sub-reddit has given into the slop gods and will burn through tokens like an IT department trying to burn its budget before year end. The best example is a person who spent more than $2000 to recreate Photoshop from scratch.

## Finding the Middle Ground

This is becoming my Maltese Falcon.
